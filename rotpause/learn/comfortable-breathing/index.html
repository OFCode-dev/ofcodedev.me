Breathing

A breathing pause should feel comfortable

Gentle breathing, clear stopping choices, and the difference between research and RotPause’s timer settings.

2 min read · Published 28 Oct 2026

AI-assisted editorial content prepared for OFCode. Sources checked 28 October 2026. Not clinically reviewed. General education, not medical advice.

Begin with comfort

You do not have to earn a pause by completing an exercise. Sit in a supported position, relax restrictive clothing if needed and let your breathing move gently. NHS guidance emphasizes comfortable breathing without forcing depth. Its suggested exercise differs from RotPause’s timer: the source is guidance for gentle practice, not validation of our exact rhythm.[1]

If a hold or a long exhale feels uncomfortable, skip it or stop and return to your normal breathing. Do not try to match an animation at the expense of comfort. Use a safe resting place, never while driving or operating machinery.

Research is not a promise about this timer

A randomized remote study compared several five-minute daily breathing practices with mindfulness meditation over a month. It reported improvements in mood, with some advantages for an exhale-focused cyclic-sighing practice. Different protocols, duration and participants limit what can be transferred to a short app exercise. That trial did not test RotPause, its 4–2–8–2 sequence or three starts per day.[2]

RotPause provides a timer and a choice to pause. It does not diagnose anxiety, measure breathing quality or establish that a particular rhythm is suitable for you. A completed-session total describes timer use, not a health outcome.

Understand the product settings

  • Each session has two seconds of preparation followed by five 16-second cycles: four seconds in, two hold, eight out, two hold. Total timer time is 82 seconds.
  • Up to three starts are available per local day. Stopping early still uses a start. This allowance is a product choice, not a medical dose or safety threshold.
  • You can stop at any time. Another kind of pause, such as putting down the phone, is also available.

Know when to stop

Stop for dizziness, faintness, pain or unexpected shortness of breath. Ask a qualified clinician before breath-holding exercises if you are pregnant or have a significant heart, lung or other medical condition. Severe breathing difficulty or sudden or persistent chest pain needs urgent medical help through your local emergency service. This article has not been clinically reviewed; the in-app safety notice remains part of using the timer.[3]
