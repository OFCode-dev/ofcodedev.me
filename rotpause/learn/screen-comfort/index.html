Screen comfort

Give your eyes a change of scene

Small screen-comfort adjustments, what the 20–20–20 reminder means, and when to seek eye care.

2 min read · Published 28 Oct 2026

AI-assisted editorial content prepared for OFCode. Sources checked 28 October 2026. Not clinically reviewed. General education, not medical advice.

Comfort is worth noticing

Long stretches of close screen work can leave eyes feeling dry, tired or uncomfortable. Digital eye strain is a description of symptoms, not a diagnosis you can make from a video count. The American Academy of Ophthalmology recommends breaks, blinking and adjustments to glare, brightness and viewing position. Persistent symptoms deserve an eye-care assessment.[1]

A phone habit and an eye symptom are related questions, but they are not interchangeable measurements. RotPause does not watch your eyes, measure blinking or tell you whether you need glasses. Its counter cannot decide when your eyes have had enough.

Use a reminder without treating it as a cure

The 20–20–20 reminder means looking about 20 feet away for 20 seconds after 20 minutes of near work. The American Optometric Association includes it in practical screen-use guidance. It is a convenient prompt to change focus, rather than a guarantee that a precise schedule will prevent eye disease.[2]

A study of 29 symptomatic computer users tested reminder software over two weeks. Symptoms improved, while most measured eye-surface, tear-film and binocular outcomes did not change; symptom benefits were not maintained after reminders stopped. The small study and short follow-up do not establish a universal cure or prove that any app reminder will produce the same result.[3]

Make the next break useful

  • Look away from near work to something farther away, and blink naturally.
  • Reduce reflections and make screen brightness comfortable relative to the room.
  • For a desk monitor, try a comfortable distance and a slightly downward gaze; enlarge text instead of leaning closer.

[1]

These are options to try, not a test you have to pass. If a reminder becomes another reason to keep checking your phone, use a simpler cue away from the screen. The useful question is whether the change makes your work or rest more comfortable.

Seek help when adjustments are not enough

If your eyes are repeatedly red, blurry, watery, light-sensitive or painful, arrange an eye-care assessment. Sudden vision loss or severe eye pain needs urgent medical attention. Do not use a break timer to delay getting help. This article is general education and has not been clinically reviewed.[1][4]
