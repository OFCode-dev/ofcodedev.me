Breathing safety

This is an optional relaxation timer. It provides no medical advice, diagnosis or treatment. The label KVRP-48 does not establish that this exact rhythm is clinically validated.

Stay comfortable

Use a comfortable, supported position. Breathe gently without forcing depth, pace or a hold. Skip a hold or stop whenever you need to. Never practice while driving or operating machinery.

If you are pregnant or have a heart, lung or other significant medical condition, ask a qualified clinician before trying breath-holding exercises.

When to stop

Stop immediately if you feel dizzy, faint, in pain or unexpectedly short of breath, and return to normal breathing. Seek medical advice for concerning symptoms. Severe breathing difficulty or sudden or persistent chest pain needs emergency medical help through your local emergency service.

How this timer works

Four seconds inhale, two hold, eight exhale, two hold. Each 1:22 session includes two seconds to prepare and five complete cycles. There is no orbiting dot. Stop stays available; leaving the exercise stops it. Up to three starts per local day, including early stops. Three full sessions total 4:06. The daily allowance is a product setting, not a medical prescription, safety threshold or evidence that this routine suits you.

Your practice history

Starts, completed sessions and recorded timer time are stored locally from this update. Earlier sessions are not reconstructed. Preparation is included, and a process interruption can omit the last interval. These are timer-use statistics, not a measurement of breathing quality, health or clinical benefit.

Sources and limits

The general guidance below informed the precautions. These organizations do not endorse RotPause, the KVRP-48 label, its 4–2–8–2 timing or the three-session limit.

  • American Heart Association: stress management — professional advice when heart or lung conditions may affect breathing exercises.
